Episode 10, Sunday Special: AI Assurance and ISO/IEC 27090, 25 October 2026

SpotifyApple PodcastsPocket Casts

This Sunday Special maps the frameworks organisations use to show their AI and AI agents are secure, from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO/IEC 42001 to OWASP's agentic Top 10, MITRE ATLAS, the EU AI Act and ASD's agent guidance. The centrepiece is ISO/IEC 27090:2026, the new international guidance on threats to AI systems, explained from ISO's public material. We also cover why agent configuration drift needs monitoring and why prompt instructions are guidance, not a security control.

Deep dive, no news segment. Content about ISO/IEC 27090 is drawn from ISO's public product page and explainer; the paywalled text has not been read.

The NIST foundation

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI 100-1): voluntary framework, January 2023, built on Govern, Map, Measure and Manage; under revision. NIST
  • Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1): July 2024 profile of risks new to or worsened by generative AI, with suggested actions. NIST
  • CAISI AI Agent Standards Initiative: launched 17 February 2026, covering agent security, identity and authorisation, and interoperability. NIST
  • Adversarial Machine Learning taxonomy (NIST AI 100-2 E2025): attack vocabulary for predictive and generative AI. NIST CSRC

The ISO family

  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023: certifiable requirements for an AI management system. ISO
  • ISO/IEC 23894:2023: guidance on AI risk management. ISO
  • ISO/IEC 42006:2025: requirements for bodies that audit and certify AI management systems. ISO

Practitioner catalogues

  • OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025): prompt injection ranked first. OWASP
  • OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026: agent goal hijack, privilege abuse, code execution, memory poisoning and rogue agents. OWASP
  • MITRE ATLAS v2026.09: 16 tactics, 120 techniques, 88 sub-techniques, 40 mitigations and 73 case studies, with new agent techniques. MITRE ATLAS, release notes

Deep dive: ISO/IEC 27090:2026

  • What it is: guidance on understanding, detecting and mitigating security threats to AI systems across the life cycle; published 14 October 2026, 56 pages, not certifiable on its own, builds on ISO/IEC 27001 and 27002. ISO, IEC webstore
  • ISO's explainer: three areas covering information security applied to AI, AI-specific threats (poisoning, extraction, evasion, inference, inversion, prompt injection, unsafe outputs) and keeping protection effective over time, including retraining risk. ISO
  • Our assessment: a strong common reference for auditors and procurement; for agent-specific controls, OWASP's agentic list and ASD's guidance currently go further. Based on public summaries, not the full text.
  • Agent configuration drift: makers can add tools, knowledge sources and instruction changes after approval; baseline, monitor, alert and re-review.
  • Prompts are guidance, not controls: system prompts can be overridden through prompt injection; enforce limits outside the model with least privilege, approval gates, harness controls and monitoring.

Regulation and the Australian angle

  • EU AI Act after the AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744): high-risk obligations moved to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028; content marking grace period ends 2 December 2026. EUR-Lex, Council of the EU timeline
  • National AI Centre: Guidance for AI Adoption: six practices replacing the earlier Voluntary AI Safety Standard guardrails. Department of Industry
  • ASD's ACSC: Careful adoption of agentic AI services: low-risk, non-sensitive tasks only; no broad or unrestricted access. cyber.gov.au
  • ASD's ACSC: Agentic AI harnesses, the layer above the model: least privilege, secure design, monitoring and logging, human oversight and accountability. cyber.gov.au

Actions for defenders and leaders

  • Build an AI inventory, including embedded AI and every agent with tool access.
  • Map training and retraining data sources; treat feedback loops as poisoning paths.
  • Least privilege and approval points for agents; govern the harness, not just the model.
  • Do not count system prompts as security controls.
  • Monitor agent configuration for drift and re-review on change.
  • Watch for high-volume or patterned queries that could signal model extraction.
  • Red-team against OWASP's agentic list and MITRE ATLAS before widening autonomy.
  • Plan a 27001 to 27090 gap review; note EU dates of 2 December 2026 and 2 December 2027.
