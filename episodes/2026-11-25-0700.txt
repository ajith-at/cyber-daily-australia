Good morning from Melbourne. You're listening to U51, the cyber and AI security brief for Wednesday the 25th of November, covering the 24 hours to about six this morning, Melbourne time.

Here's what's making headlines. Researchers say a heavily automated attacker compromised Check Point firewall management servers at 22 organisations in 17 countries, Australia among them. ASX-listed Peninsula Energy has halted trading after a cyber incident at its uranium mine in the United States. Broadcom has fixed another critical authentication bypass in VMware vCenter, along with flaws that could let attackers run code on ESX hosts. And Britain's cyber agency says AI-assisted attacks have risen markedly since September, mostly by exploiting weaknesses organisations have carried for years.

Our lead story is about the servers that run the firewalls. The threat intelligence firm GreyNoise says an actor it tracks as GT13 compromised 24 Check Point Security Management servers belonging to at least 22 organisations across 17 countries and territories, including Australia. The victims include government bodies, financial services firms, and companies in energy, transport, mining and manufacturing, with no clear sign of a single target sector. GreyNoise watched the activity from the 17th of October until the 2nd of November, when its visibility ended, and believes the campaign is still running because the attacker kept persistent access. It doesn't know how the attacker first got in. Once inside, the actor exploited an authentication bypass in Check Point's SmartConsole management software, stole firewall databases, credentials, configuration data and internal certificate authority files, unloaded security policies and tried to move on to other systems. That flaw was patched in July and is already on the United States exploited list. On the 26th of October it ran a bulk certificate operation across all 24 servers at once. It routed its traffic through a hijacked file server and VPN exit nodes. GreyNoise believes the actor used large language models and agent tooling for scanning, exploitation and tool building, pointing to odd stalls in the timeline that look like an automated pipeline tripping over itself. That is the firm's assessment, and it hasn't been independently confirmed. There is no attribution. A management server holds the keys to every gateway it manages. If you run Check Point, make sure July's Jumbo Hotfix is installed, restrict management access and Trusted Clients to known addresses, look for unexpected certificate operations, policy unloads and admin sessions from unfamiliar addresses, and check the indicators in the show notes.

Australian focus.

ASX-listed Peninsula Energy asked the ASX for a trading halt on Monday after a cyber incident late on Friday at its Lance uranium project in Wyoming. The company told the exchange the incident breached the project's operating system, that its administrative systems in the United States and Australia weren't affected, and that operations would be temporarily disrupted. It hasn't said which systems were hit, whether uranium production stopped, or whether any data was taken. The halt runs until normal trading resumes today or the company releases a fuller announcement, so expect more detail shortly.

Silverstripe, the New Zealand-developed content management system whose users include state government agencies and banks, shipped fixes on Tuesday for two critical flaws. The worse one, rated 9.8, lets an anonymous visitor submit a standard form, such as the login form, and make the site load and run a template from a server the attacker controls. It depends on a PHP setting that is on by default. The second, rated 9.1, lets an attacker sign in to any account with no password set, including the default administrator account, and a strong admin password doesn't protect you on its own. Neither has a CVE number yet. Patch now; if you can't, turn off that PHP setting where it isn't needed, restrict the admin paths to trusted addresses and audit passwordless accounts. Details are in the show notes.

Snowy's Camping Goods, the Adelaide-founded outdoor retailer, has warned customers that malicious code ran on its website from the 5th of October to the 16th of November, targeting details typed in at checkout, sign-in and account creation. Names, email addresses and phone numbers may have been captured, along with passwords for people who signed in or registered. The company says there's no sign card details were involved, it has reset the affected passwords and it has told the privacy regulator. So far this rests on the customer notice as reported in the press. If you shop there and reused that password elsewhere, change it.

In Canberra, Labor has struck a deal with the Greens to pass the Digital Duty of Care Bill through the Senate. The amendments give Australians a private right to sue social media companies for harm suffered online and raise the maximum penalties to as much as 5 per cent of a platform's global revenue. The Senate backed the bill in principle on Tuesday and was still working through amendments; Senate changes will also need the House's agreement. It's online safety law rather than cyber security law, but platform risk and compliance teams will be reading it closely.

Global threats.

Broadcom has patched 23 vulnerabilities across VMware ESX, vCenter, Workstation, Fusion, VMware Tools and the Avi Load Balancer. The headline is another critical authentication bypass in vCenter's directory service, rated 9.8, which lets anyone with network access to vCenter get in without credentials. Broadcom says it is a separate bug from the directory service zero-day that a suspected China-linked group exploited, which we covered last week. The same advisory fixes flaws that could let an attacker with network access to an ESX host run code on it. Nothing in this batch is reported as exploited yet. If you already applied last week's vCenter 8 update, Broadcom says that release covers vCenter 8, but vCenter 9 and every ESX host still need this one, and VMware Tools inside your virtual machines needs its own update. Advisory details are in the show notes.

Radware, the Israeli application security and DDoS protection company, has disclosed that on the 19th of November an outsider reached an internal management interface for its cloud web application firewall that was exposed to the internet without authentication. In a root cause report seen by BleepingComputer, Radware says customer contact details were potentially accessed, and for a subset of customers, the credentials they configured for exporting logs to their own Amazon S3, Azure Blob or SFTP storage. It detected the access about ten hours later. Affected customers should rotate those log export credentials and check their storage logs back to the 19th. A security vendor leaving an unauthenticated API on the open internet is the sort of irony nobody enjoys, least of all its customers.

The ShinyHunters extortion brand says it is back. A new leak site appeared on Monday, signed with the group's old PGP keys, though BleepingComputer notes those keys have passed through many hands, so they don't prove who is behind it. Separately, Okta's threat intelligence team says a voice phishing cluster that used to publish victims on the ShinyHunters site has moved to a new leak site called INCEPTION since the 16th of November. It is still phoning staff, steering them to fake login pages and talking them into approving multi-factor prompts. Help desks and staff should treat unexpected calls about logins with suspicion.

Wordfence reports that six WordPress plugins from the developer WP connect, which sync sites with Airtable, Notion, Brevo and Contact Form 7, shipped with a backdoor after the developer's WordPress account was compromised. The poisoned releases let a remote server run code on the site. They have a combined install base of more than 3,600, all six are now closed, and there's no fixed version, so uninstall them and run a full malware scan.

The web hosting billing platform WHMCS has fixed an SQL injection flaw in its shopping cart that needs no login and can expose customer records and administrator password hashes. Two other flaws need a logged-in client, one of which can lead to code execution through email campaigns and needs extra clean-up steps beyond updating. No attacks have been reported. Fixed versions and the clean-up steps are in the show notes.

Two breaches in North Asia. South Korea's state power company KEPCO says an attacker accessed its electricity billing platform and its procurement system, exposing details of more than 1.5 million people. And McDonald's Japan says attackers got into its app database, exposing purchase history, IP addresses and device location data, though not names or email addresses.

Finally in this section, a quick catalogue update. The Drupal security team now says the installer flaw we covered on Saturday was exploited on live sites from about six on Friday morning, Melbourne time. The United States cyber agency has added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue, along with the Optimizely and Idunno AntiCsrf flaws we've already reported on, a day after adding the MONAI Label and N-able Mail Assure flaws. If you run Drupal and haven't updated or locked down the installer, update now and check the site for signs of compromise.

AI security.

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre says it has seen a marked rise in AI-assisted attacks since September, with organisations across sectors reporting incidents involving what it calls agentic offensive frameworks: software built for red teaming, turned against real targets and paired with cheap open models or illegal API resellers. Its point is that none of the weaknesses being exploited are new. The agents are going after credentials committed to code repositories, infrastructure misconfigurations and identity settings that have drifted, and they do it faster and more persistently, at lower cost. The advice is familiar: patch internet-facing systems at the pace the attackers test them, fix configuration drift and keep error messages from giving anything away. Old technical debt, it turns out, now accrues interest by the minute.

On the defensive side, OpenAI and Cisco have released Shirudo, an open-source Linux tool that lets AI agents write temporary runtime protections, built on the kernel's eBPF technology, that block the behaviour an exploit relies on while legitimate traffic carries on. An operator approves each shield before it goes live, and the idea is to buy time to test and roll out the real patch without taking services down. It's a vendor launch and an early one, so the useful question for defenders is how well those shields hold up against exploit variants.

Industry.

Queensland is advertising for a new general manager of cyber security, effectively the whole-of-government chief information security officer, after the previous holder left earlier this year. The government links the role to preparing for significant cyber incidents and to the Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Applications close on the 3rd of December.

Outlook and patch priorities.

Critical first. Check Point management servers: hunt for the activity in the GreyNoise report and lock down management access. Drupal: it is being exploited, so update or secure the installer today. Silverstripe: apply the new fixes before someone works the exploit out from them. VMware: vCenter 9, all ESX hosts and VMware Tools need this week's advisory. And remove the six backdoored WP connect plugins.

Next, high. WHMCS operators should update and follow the email campaign clean-up steps, and Radware cloud firewall customers should rotate their log export credentials.

To watch over the next day or two: Peninsula Energy's promised announcement to the ASX, likely today; whether exploit code appears for the new vCenter and Silverstripe flaws; and the United States federal deadline for the MONAI Label and N-able Mail Assure flaws, which falls on Thursday, United States time.
