Good morning from Melbourne. It's Thursday the 19th of November, and this is U51, your cyber and AI security brief for the 24 hours to about six this morning, Melbourne time.

Top of the brief this morning. Researchers have disclosed critical flaws in the web server NGINX that can let an attacker run code without logging in, and fixes are out. Two poisoned releases of Microsoft's AI agent framework on the Python Package Index carried a credential stealer. Optimizely has explained how its web testing service was hijacked, and an Australian bank, a telco and a super fund are among the affected sites. Fixes for the Java4Shell flaw have landed, with the United States cyber agency urging immediate patching. And United States intelligence officials say the recent run of major outages was not a cyberattack.

We start with NGINX, the open source web server and reverse proxy that sits in front of a very large share of the world's websites and APIs. On Wednesday, the NGINX maintainers released fixes for ten vulnerabilities, two of them in the HTTP/2 code and rated critical by the researchers who found them. The first, CVE-2026-123007, is memory corruption in HTTP/2 flow control that dates back to 2015. The second, CVE-2026-123633, is a use-after-free, also in HTTP/2. Wiz Research says both can be reached by an unauthenticated attacker through normal TLS processing, with no backend application or third-party module needed, and that it built working proof-of-concept exploits against common configurations in its lab. Rapid7 has published its own analysis. No exploitation in the wild has been reported so far. The fixed open source releases and the NGINX Plus builds are listed in the show notes. The same release also fixes flaws in HTTP/3, the mail proxy, the resolver and TLS 1.3 handling, so the upgrade is worth doing even where HTTP/2 is already off. If you can't upgrade straight away, the researchers suggest temporarily turning off HTTP/2. Don't forget the appliances, ingress controllers and container images that bundle their own copy, and remember to restart after upgrading.

Australian focus.

There are new details on the Optimizely incident we led with yesterday, including the root cause and the Australian sites involved. In a customer incident report, Optimizely says an attacker exploited a flaw in the account signup flow of its Web Experimentation product to act as administrator users on customer accounts, without needing their passwords, and then edited project code to load a card skimmer. The company says it closed the flaw within just over eight hours of the first malicious change and removed the code the same day. Sites with a strong Content Security Policy blocked the skimmer, and cards entered into hosted payment fields, such as those from Stripe or Adyen, were out of its reach. iTnews reports that the researcher's list of affected organisations includes Vodafone, HSBC Australia, Rest Super, TPG Telecom, Betashares and Compare Club. TPG Telecom, which owns Vodafone, says it has paused Optimizely, has found no evidence its systems or customer data were compromised, and that the affected pages had no payment or login functions. Report URI traced about 7,000 US dollars to the attacker's wallets, but says it cannot see how many cards or cookies were taken. If you paid on an affected site on the 16th or 17th, keep an eye on your card statements, and update older iPhones and iPads.

Global threats.

The Java4Shell story has moved on since yesterday. Oracle's November update and the OpenJDK fixes are now out, and the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, known as CISA, has issued an alert urging organisations to patch without delay. CISA's advice is to inventory every Java runtime, including copies bundled inside third-party applications, appliances and container images, and to patch internet-facing systems first. There are no reports of exploitation yet, but the researchers have published proof-of-concept code for the memory corruption itself.

Apache has warned that a critical flaw in Roller, its Java-based blogging server, is being exploited. Tracked as CVE-2026-129448, it lets an unauthenticated attacker run code through the XML-RPC interface, because the server deserialises data before checking who sent it. Roller is a niche product, but if you run it, upgrade to the fixed release in the show notes now.

In Japan, the research institute RIKEN has taken the Fugaku supercomputer offline after unauthorised access between the 10th and 13th of November. RIKEN says the attacker got in through a public operating status dashboard and may have taken names, email addresses, user IDs and stored SSH keys or other credentials belonging to up to 4,597 users of Fugaku and its RIKYU AI system. It has found no evidence so far that research data was taken. Anyone who stored keys on Fugaku should revoke and replace them.

The extortion group n0n, which claimed Expedia last week, has named 59 organisations on its leak site, according to the tracker Ransomnews, including Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase. Mastercard has not commented. Visa told Cybernews the incident began at a third-party service provider in the Asia Pacific region, that its own systems were not affected and that there is no sign card numbers were involved. JPMorgan says a recent service disruption was caused by a software bug and that it has no evidence of an attack. The group has published no proof for most of these claims, so treat them as unverified for now.

Two more extortion items. Osaki Electric, a Japanese maker of smart electricity meters, told the Tokyo Stock Exchange on Wednesday that a ransomware attack has halted production while it investigates. And the ransomware group known as The Gentlemen has listed Nvidia on its leak site with a countdown, but has posted no files or other evidence, and Nvidia has not confirmed any breach. For now that is a claim, not an incident.

AI security.

Two malicious releases of agent-framework, the Python package for Microsoft Agent Framework, were published to the Python Package Index on Tuesday, United States time, about half an hour apart. Each added a small startup file that runs every time Python starts in that environment, whether or not the package is used, and collects secrets from environment variables, credential files, other processes, Azure managed identities and continuous integration pipelines, including GitHub Actions tokens in the second release. Both releases were pulled within about an hour, and the earlier release is clean. The exact versions are in the show notes. Wiz says the attacker appears to have used a compromised certificate for Microsoft's internal release pipeline, and that the tooling identified itself as Cairn, an open source AI penetration testing framework. Microsoft has not yet said how that access was obtained. The package sees almost 200,000 downloads a month. If any build or developer machine installed either release, treat it as compromised and rotate every credential it could reach.

At its Ignite conference, Microsoft has announced MAI Cyber 2 Flash, a low-cost model for finding, confirming and fixing vulnerabilities, available first through its own security products, alongside new coding models in GitHub Copilot. And according to Forbes, OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber model, working with the security firm Calif, found about 35 flaws across Apple's platforms, including an exploit that gets around some Lockdown Mode protections. Apple has fixed many of them, but the full list rests on the companies' own account. Between Java4Shell, which Calif also found with an AI agent, and this, the pace of machine-found bugs is now setting defenders' patch calendars.

Industry and policy.

In Washington, the Director of National Intelligence, Jay Clayton, told the House Homeland Security Committee there is no indication that the recent wave of outages is related, coordinated or the work of a state-sponsored actor. The incidents include a nationwide flight ground stop, a long Microsoft Outlook outage, a reported outage of the National Security Agency's classified network, and problems at JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines and Apple. A senior official put them down to separate upgrades, patching and hardware changes, though said it was hard to say how far AI was involved. Vice President JD Vance cancelled a conference appearance to attend meetings on the outages. The lesson for everyone else is an old one: rushed change is its own risk, and change control still matters when the patch queue is long.

Outlook and patch priorities.

First, NGINX: find every instance, including the ones bundled inside other products, upgrade to the fixed release and restart, or turn off HTTP/2 until you can. Second, if agent-framework is anywhere in your Python estate, check for the malicious releases and rotate credentials. Third, finish your Java inventory and apply the November fixes, internet-facing first. Fourth, Apache Roller, if you have it. The four CISA catalogue deadlines on Tuesday, for Outlook, Cisco BroadWorks, Openwave and Chrome, are close, and the Puppet deadline follows on the 1st of December. Expect proof-of-concept work on NGINX to spread over the next day or two.
