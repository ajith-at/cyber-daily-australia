The weekend in brief. Saturday's edition led with the Binance breach, put at about six point three billion US dollars, and traced by the exchange to a compromised engineer's laptop. We also covered Ivanti closing the last two holes in an exploited Connect Secure VPN chain, a tampered Claude Code extension served through Cursor's extension marketplace, and attacks on fresh Drupal Commerce and WordPress flaws.

Sunday's edition was a Sunday Special, Episode Seventeen, on the Australian Signals Directorate's Annual Cyber Threat Report for twenty twenty-five to twenty-six. Three things to take from it. Patch in days, not weeks, because some flaws are now exploited within forty-eight hours of a fix or advice. Attackers increasingly log in rather than break in, so stolen and reused passwords matter as much as unpatched software. And medium-sized businesses took the sharpest rise in reported costs, up almost half on the year before. ASD's answer is to assume a breach will happen and get the basics right. The full episode and its notes are on u51 dot org.

Now this morning's headlines. The FBI has formally named North Korea as the thief behind the Binance breach. A flaw with a perfect severity score in Canonical's LXD and MicroCloud lets an attacker log in as a trusted client using only that client's public certificate. The hosting platform Northflank has told every customer to rotate every secret, after stolen AI keys were used. And the blockchain analysis firm Chainalysis has disclosed its own breach.

First, Binance, with one significant development. On Saturday we said the attribution to North Korea came from blockchain analytics firms, not a government. That has changed. The FBI, with the US Secret Service and the US tax agency's criminal investigators, says North Korea's TraderTraitor group stole more than six billion US dollars from the exchange on or about the twenty-ninth of October. The agencies say the group is converting the haul mainly into Ethereum, ready to launder it through decentralised finance and instant-swap services, and they've published the wallet addresses involved, asking exchanges, node operators and analytics firms to block them. Elliptic now puts the total at about six point four billion dollars across eleven blockchains, with close to two point nine billion frozen. Binance started restoring withdrawals in phases yesterday. If your organisation touches crypto flows in any way, load those addresses into your screening now. They're linked in the show notes.

Next, a flaw for anyone running containers or virtual machines on Linux. Canonical's LXD manages both, and MicroCloud builds small clusters on top of it. Clients prove who they are with a certificate, and the server checks it against a list of trusted ones. The bug is that the server looked at every certificate the client sent in its chain, not just the one the client actually holds the key for. So an attacker who could reach the management interface, and had a copy of a trusted client's public certificate, could slip that certificate into the chain and be accepted as that client. Public certificates are not secret, which is rather the point of them. With a privileged account, Canonical says, that can mean root on the host. It scores ten out of ten, and no attacks have been reported. The fixed versions are listed in the show notes. Refresh the snaps, then check the version that's actually installed, because the fixes rolled out to the older release channels gradually. Until every host is done, keep the management interface off networks that don't need it.

The cloud hosting platform Northflank has had a breach that reached customers' AI accounts. After an alert from the security firm Wiz on Friday, Northflank told every customer to assume all their environment variables had been decrypted, and to rotate every secret stored on the platform. Customers then reported their AI provider keys being used by someone else. Northflank hasn't said how the attacker got in or how many customers are affected, so details are still emerging. It has released secret rotation for its database add-ons and switched on audit logs for everyone, free accounts included. If you use it, revoke each key at the provider that issued it, because changing the value inside Northflank doesn't stop a copy someone already holds. Then check your AI usage and billing from the thirtieth of October, and set spending limits. Stolen AI keys turn into somebody else's compute bill very quickly, and the bill is still yours.

A short update on Polymarket, the prediction market whose site loaded a hijacked script last week. Polymarket says it is refunding about two point seven million dollars to seven hundred and twenty-six wallets, and close to four million in total once losses during the trading pause are included. The provider of the script, DebugBear, says the attacker got control of its address records through its domain registrar. Those are its early findings. For any site that loads someone else's code where users sign or pay, pinning the exact file with an integrity check, so the browser rejects a changed version, is the control that would have caught this.

Android users have a reason to install October's update. A researcher has published working code for a flaw called SMS of Death. One malformed text message, which never reaches the inbox, crashes the phone's cellular service and drops calls in progress, emergency calls included. Send enough of them and some phones get stuck restarting. The researcher says Google fixed it in October for Android fourteen to seventeen, though it isn't listed in Google's October bulletin, and Android twelve and thirteen haven't received a fix. That's the researcher's account, not Google's. Check your phone's security patch level, and if a work fleet still has older Android devices, this is one more reason to retire them.

And a look inside a ransomware operation, courtesy of the operator. The threat intelligence firm SOCRadar found a file server left open to the internet by someone behind the n0n extortion group, holding more than seventeen hundred files. They show attacks on twenty-three organisations in fourteen countries, and every break-in it documented used weak or stolen passwords. It found scanning for exposed remote desktop logins, automated stuffing of FortiGate VPN logins, and cloud keys left exposed. It found no software exploits at all. The operator spent more than seven thousand US dollars on AI services, using an open-source coding agent to write and debug hundreds of scripts, and in some cases deleted data and backups as well as encrypting them. This rests on a single firm's investigation. The useful point is the one the operator proved by leaving its own server open: the AI helped with the scripting, and the way in was still a login.

On to AI security, and Chainalysis. The firm whose analysts helped trace the Binance theft has disclosed its own breach. It says an outside actor used what it calls offensive agentic techniques to get into its systems and internal tools this month. Names, email addresses, user IDs and role labels of customers' users were accessed. Chainalysis says it has found no sign that investigation content, passwords, multi-factor secrets or session tokens were reached, and that the incident is unrelated to Binance. It hasn't said which weaknesses were exploited or how many records were taken, and the AI description is its own, with no technical detail behind it yet. Whatever tooling the attacker used, the weaknesses were in Chainalysis's systems, and those are what it says it's patching. Users should switch on two-factor authentication and treat any unexpected password reset with suspicion.

OpenAI has fixed a flaw in ChatGPT's shared conversations. Researchers at the firm Parameter showed that a booby-trapped shared chat could run their code inside the session of anyone signed in who opened it, and read that person's private conversations. In the most reliable version the victim clicked Preview once, and in a later version simply opening the link was enough. The route ran through a maths input component that accepted messages from other frames on the page without checking where they came from. Parameter reported it on the eighteenth of October and OpenAI fixed it eight days later, rating it in its most serious category. There's nothing for users to do now, beyond the usual caution with shared links from people you don't know.

And a reminder of what AI-assisted code review can find in the software everything depends on. The AI security testing firm Hacktron, reported by iTnews, pointed its AI-assisted auditing tool at the software behind GNU Savannah, the Free Software Foundation's code forge, which hosts Bash and the core Linux command-line tools. An email address with a command wrapped in backticks, which email standards allow, was passed straight to the system shell when a user tested their signing key. From there the researchers made themselves administrators, could have added a key with permission to push code into upstream Bash under its maintainer's name, and got root through an unpatched kernel. They stopped before touching the repository. The Foundation has fixed all twenty-six issues they reported and found no sign that the supply chain was compromised. Bash releases are signed by the maintainer, so a planted change would also have had to get past him. If your code builds a shell command out of user input anywhere, this is the case to show the team.

In industry news, Cloudflare's quarterly report counts eighteen point two million network-layer denial-of-service attacks between July and September, up thirty-eight per cent on the previous quarter. The growth is in short, small bursts of the kind sold by attack-for-hire services, while the very largest attacks, above a terabit per second, fell by more than two thirds. Small attacks are cheap to launch, so defences need to handle many short ones, not just the occasional huge one.

Finally, the outlook and patch priorities. Today, US time, is the federal deadline for the Ivanti Connect Secure privilege-escalation flaw, and that deadline comes with a check for compromise as well as the patch. Patch LXD and MicroCloud anywhere the management interface listens on a network. Northflank customers, rotate every secret at its source. Install October's Android updates, and plan for older Android devices that won't get one. Expect more on where the Binance funds go, and more detail from Northflank.
