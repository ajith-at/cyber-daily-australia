Good morning from Melbourne, and welcome to U51, the cyber and AI security brief for Friday the 20th of November, covering the 24 hours to about six this morning, Melbourne time.

Here are the headlines. Broadcom has patched a critical authentication bypass in VMware vCenter that Google says a suspected China-linked group exploited as a zero-day in September. The United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency has added four exploited flaws to its catalogue, including the LiteLLM AI gateway and the end-of-life Apache Axis library. The European Commission has confirmed a cyber campaign run with a publicly available AI penetration testing tool. And PHP and Ruby have both shipped security releases fixing critical memory and request-handling bugs.

Our lead today is VMware vCenter. Broadcom's advisory, published yesterday, fixes CVE-2026-123890, an authentication bypass in the VMware Directory Service rated 9.8 out of 10. Anyone with network access to vCenter can bypass authentication, and there is no workaround. Google's Mandiant reported the flaw, and Google's threat intelligence group says the actor it tracks as Armory Castle, formerly known as UNC5221 and assessed as likely China-nexus, exploited it as a zero-day in early September. The group first came in through a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day, moved to an internal Linux appliance, then used a custom tool called Bonesaw against vCenter to steal its directory database, hijack the single sign-on token service and create its own single sign-on administrator account. Fixes cover vCenter 8.0, 9.0 and 9.1 and VMware Cloud Foundation 5, and the exact fixed builds are in the show notes. vCenter 7.0 gets no public patch; Broadcom tells customers on extended support to contact it. The same advisory fixes three flaws in SDDC Manager, the Cloud Foundation management appliance, and one in VMware Tools for Windows. Because vCenter controls the whole virtual estate, this one goes to the top of the patch queue, along with a hunt for unexpected single sign-on administrator accounts.

Australian focus.

Woolworths customers are still waiting for an explanation after digital gift cards were drained of up to five hundred dollars each. The ABC reports that affected customers bought Everyday WISH e-gift cards through the Everyday Rewards app or website during a recent four per cent discount promotion, then saw purchases at stores they never visited. Woolworths apologised on Monday, says it is investigating and is issuing replacement cards, but has declined to say what caused the losses or whether the problem is contained. Experts quoted by the ABC suggest an enumeration attack, where bots guess card numbers against an online balance checker, or fake balance-check sites; neither has been confirmed, so treat the cause as unknown for now. If you hold a Woolworths e-gift card, check its transaction history.

Global threats.

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, or CISA, added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue, and three of them carry a federal deadline of this Saturday, the 21st of November, United States time. The most consequential is CVE-2026-90436 in Apache Axis, an old Java library for SOAP web services that is no longer supported. A crafted SOAP fault message makes Axis load and create a class the attacker chooses, before any authentication runs. Rapid7 says it caught attackers exploiting it against an Atlassian Confluence Data Center server on the 13th of November, where the Remote API setting was switched on. That setting is off by default. Within minutes the attackers were querying Confluence's database. SecurityWeek reports that within four hours they were in a cloud data warehouse, and about twelve hours in they had signed in to Microsoft 365 with an integration service account. Rapid7 adds that the scripted, task-numbered commands suggest AI-assisted automation. Apache will not ship a fixed Axis release, so the remedy depends on the product that bundles it: Confluence users should upgrade to the fixed version or to release 10, or switch off the Remote API, and N-able's latest N-central hotfix disables the legacy interface that exposes it.

The same batch adds the REDCap research data platform, used widely by universities and hospitals. Its flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker run code on a self-hosted server through a public survey link, and the fixed releases are in the show notes. The last of the three is a 2024 Synology DiskStation Manager flaw that allows remote code execution, now confirmed as exploited.

Two follow-ups on flaws we covered this week. The NGINX research team at Xint has now given its findings a name, NGINX2Root, covering five critical memory bugs, and shows them chained with an unpatched Linux kernel flaw to reach root on the host, including from inside a container. Rapid7 has published a working exploit for one of the HTTP/2 bugs, although it needs very specific conditions and around 128 gigabytes of traffic. No exploitation has been reported yet. If you upgraded, restart the service rather than reloading it, because a reload does not replace the running program. On Java4Shell, GreyNoise and watchTowr now see scanning and blind exploitation attempts, though GreyNoise reports no successful intrusions on its sensors so far.

And JPMorgan Chase has now confirmed that third-party vendors were attacked and that marketing data, including some customer names and email addresses, was accessed. The bank says this is unrelated to its outage earlier this week. Mastercard says a limited number of email addresses from one benefit program were exposed. Cybernews puts the leaked records at around sixteen thousand for JPMorgan, but those counts come from the leak itself and have not been confirmed by the companies.

AI security.

The LiteLLM entry in CISA's catalogue matters for anyone running AI gateways. LiteLLM is a popular open-source proxy that sits between applications and model providers and holds their API keys. The flaw, CVE-2026-40217, is in a guardrail test feature that runs user-supplied Python inside a home-made sandbox, and that sandbox can be escaped. In the default Docker image the proxy runs as root. An attacker needs a proxy admin key, but Zenity's honeypot research shows attackers simply guessing default master keys, then using the escape to dump environment variables holding provider keys. That is how stolen keys end up in LLMjacking, where criminals resell or run up bills on someone else's model access. Upgrade to the fixed release listed in the show notes, block the guardrail test endpoint at the edge, replace any default master key and rotate the provider keys the proxy holds. Federal agencies have until the 2nd of December.

The European Commission has confirmed that it was hit by what it calls a detected cyber campaign, first spotted on the 22nd of October. According to Politico, as reported by The Register, defenders saw the attackers using ARTEX, a publicly available penetration testing framework that runs fleets of AI agents. Staff systems for payslips, timesheets, leave and medical reimbursements were offline for more than ten days, and the translation service was also affected. The Commission says it found no evidence of a broad breach of staff data and no ransom demand, and the investigation continues. CERT-EU, which protects European Union institutions, warned on the 5th of November that it had seen ARTEX-assisted attacks, and the FBI has counted more than sixteen hundred ARTEX control consoles exposed online. The developer pulled the code in October, but copies of the project are still circulating.

Industry and policy.

PHP has released security updates for every supported branch, fixing 18 vulnerabilities. The most severe is a request smuggling flaw in PHP-FPM rated 9.2, which can let an unauthenticated attacker run PHP code, but only where the web server reuses its connections to PHP-FPM. Standard NGINX and Apache setups that do not reuse those connections are not affected. Several other fixes address use-after-free and session-handling bugs that could lead to code execution. The PHP team says the critical issues need non-default or uncommon setups. Ruby has also shipped updates fixing seven bugs in its regular expression and string case-conversion code, including critical heap overflows. Versions for both languages are in the show notes.

Palo Alto Networks has agreed to buy Applied Compute, a platform for tuning and serving open-weight AI models, in an all-stock deal valued at about four and a half billion US dollars. Palo Alto says it wants to help enterprises run open models securely in public and sovereign clouds, and expects the deal to close in its 2027 financial year. And in Washington, Senators Maria Cantwell and Mike Rounds have asked the White House's AI task force for urgent national action on defensive AI tools and security standards for autonomous agents, warning that AI-enabled attackers are now finding and exploiting software flaws faster than organisations can patch them.

Outlook and patch priorities.

First, VMware vCenter: patch now, and look for unfamiliar single sign-on administrator accounts and unusual activity on the vCenter directory service. Organisations still on vCenter 7.0 should treat it as exposed and restrict network access to it. Second, the CISA catalogue additions: Confluence servers with the Remote API enabled, self-hosted REDCap, Synology devices and LiteLLM proxies, and for LiteLLM rotate the provider keys as well as patching. Third, PHP-FPM setups that reuse connections, then the routine PHP and Ruby updates. Over the next two days, expect more exploit code for NGINX and Java4Shell, and a reminder that a feature labelled "test" still runs in production if it is switched on.
