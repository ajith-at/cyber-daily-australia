Episode 32: Cursor traces its poisoned extension to a Fireworks key, AI agents run Chinese intrusions and Airtasker support data stolen, 16 November 2026

SpotifyApple PodcastsPocket Casts

Cursor says the trojanised Claude Code extension in its marketplace traces back to an access key exposed in the Fireworks AI breach. Unit 42 reports Chinese-speaking attackers using the open-source Cairn AI framework to run largely autonomous intrusions, succeeding at 10 organisations. Airtasker confirms theft of customer support data, and AWS recovered from a three-hour sign-in and connectivity incident in US-EAST-1.

Weekend recap

  • Episode 31, Sunday Special: Open-Weight AI Models, Use and Risk. Opened with the Microsoft 365 outage resolution and outage-themed phishing. Five takeaways: self-hosting moves the black box inside your perimeter but doesn't make it trustworthy; know exactly what you run (approved sources, verified provenance, safe formats, a register with an accountable owner); test the specific version, treating fine-tuned, quantised, merged or abliterated copies as different models; put controls outside the model (default-deny egress, isolation, least-privilege tools, human approval for irreversible actions, enforce on the action, not the reasoning trace); monitor, rehearse and be willing to say no. Episode 31 notes
  • Episode 30 (Saturday). Check Point Gaia zero-day chained with PHP2Shell (TSGA backdoor, ESXi wiper), US disruption of North Korea's FaceStealer and move to seize more than US$1.2 billion stolen from Binance, PostgreSQL 52 fixes, Quantum Systems cyberattack. Episode 30 notes
  • US FAA nationwide ground stop not a cyberattack, officials say. Attributed to a communications system problem; FBI reportedly found no sign of hacking (press reports of officials). Sources: https://www.securityweek.com/no-signs-of-hacking-in-faa-ground-stop-as-fbi-reportedly-probes-software-update/ ; https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6wy9lynq4qno

Lead story

Cursor: stolen Fireworks AI key led to trojanised Claude Code extension Attacker used a Cursor-issued access key exposed by Fireworks to copy a read-only bucket of coding agent traces (non-Privacy Mode sessions, mostly 2025); unredacted credentials (under 0.01% of customers) led to some SaaS tools, archived non-production repositories, support data and credentials sent to support. On 29 Oct the malicious extension (versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313) was placed on the marketplace cache for about 11 hours; potentially at most 5,000 users; Open VSX never had the malware. Inference moved off Fireworks; no malicious activity seen since 1 Nov. Uber confirmed theft via a service provider TechCrunch reports was Fireworks; Notion confirmed data stolen in the Fireworks hack. Source: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/stolen-fireworks-ai-key-led-to-cursor.html

Australian focus

Airtasker customer support data stolen from third-party system Access identified and closed 10 Nov; contact details and support ticket content, and in a small number of cases ID documents, card numbers or passwords customers typed into tickets. Stored account card details not involved. Airtasker says the intruder appeared to be preparing a payment scam; affected users emailed. Source: https://support.airtasker.com/hc/en-gb/articles/64280722804505-November-2026-Third-Party-Security-Incident

Expedia (Wotif owner) confirms attack via third-party data platform provider n0n extortion group claims hundreds of millions of records; unverified, and Expedia has not linked its incident to the claim. Cyber Daily reports Australian email addresses in posted material. Sources: https://skift.com/2026/11/12/expedia-confirms-cyberattack-through-third-party-vendor/ ; https://thetechboom.com/2026/11/14/expedia-breach-third-party-vendor-wotif-australia/

Global threats

AWS US-EAST-1 sign-in and connectivity incident (resolved) Between 3:05am and 6:10am PST 15 Nov (about 10:05pm Sunday to 1:10am Monday AEDT): sign-up errors, IAM Identity Center authentication failures (console, Kiro, Amazon Connect and other dependent services) and connectivity issues; a subset of Network Load Balancers in use1-az1 affected longer. Root cause not yet disclosed. Source: https://health.aws.amazon.com/health/status

TSMC confirms current and former employee data leaked Data matched information stored with a cloud service provider; no evidence of compromise of TSMC systems. Forum seller "SmithBianl" claims 46,000 records (6.9GB), including national IDs and banking details (seller's claim). Source: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/tsmc-employee-data-breach-linked-to-cloud-provider/

UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713 exploited (CISA KEV, due 16 Nov) labeled-response module fails to escape the IRCv3 label tag; any user on a network of two or more linked servers can inject server-to-server commands. Affected: 6.0.0 to before 6.2.7.1. Fixed: 6.2.7.1 (released 7 Nov; spamfilter workaround available). Exploitation from 8 Nov. Sources: https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-exploit-unrealircd-flaw-that.html ; https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog

Maersk confirms data theft from acquired legacy system Operations unaffected; WhiteArmor claims about 100GB from the LF Logistics platform and demands US$5 million (unverified). Sources: https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/11/11/it-security-incident ; https://www.securityweek.com/maersk-confirms-data-theft-amid-5-million-extortion-attempt/

AI security

Unit 42: Cairn AI framework used in autonomous intrusions (CL-STA-1238) 25 operations against at least 19 organisations (Aug to Sep 2026) using Cairn with Zhipu GLM and DeepSeek models; access or data theft at 10, including credentials, over a million personal records at one victim, persistent admin accounts and an edited standards-ballot document. Semiconductor, US defence policy and civil society targets. China-nexus assessed with moderate confidence. IoCs in the Unit 42 report. Source: https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/cairn-framework-ai-autonomous-attacks/

Microsoft removes remaining eMudhra roots from 16 November Windows, Edge, WebView2 and Azure Linux; disconnected systems need manual trust list updates. Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2026/10/microsoft-response-to-compromised-emudhra-certificate-authority-keys
