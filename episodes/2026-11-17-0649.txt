From Melbourne, this is U51 with your cyber and AI security brief for Tuesday the 17th of November, covering the 24 hours to about seven this morning, Melbourne time.

Here's what's making headlines. Coles is investigating a cyber security incident and says it has found no evidence to date that customer, team or supplier data was compromised. Google's threat intelligence group says attackers are now running intrusions with open-source AI agent frameworks and automating exploits built from software patches. A Linux kernel flaw called NoisyTCP, eleven years old, lets an unauthenticated attacker crash exposed servers. And Microsoft has traced last week's global Microsoft 365 outage to a software bug introduced in July.

Australian focus.

Coles Group told the Australian Securities Exchange yesterday that it is investigating a cyber security incident. The company says that, based on its investigation to date, it has not identified any evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised or removed from its systems. Coles is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and outside cyber security experts to contain and secure its IT environment, and says stores and online shopping are operating as normal, although a small number of customers are having problems with Liquorland click-and-collect. The ABC reports that Coles' site also lists gift cards as working normally and no indication of any impact on Flybuys accounts. Posts on Reddit claiming to be from staff say some in-store stock tools are offline; Coles hasn't confirmed that. Coles has not said which systems were affected, how the attackers got in, whether ransomware was involved, or when the incident began. As of this morning, its updates page is unchanged since yesterday morning, and no claim of responsibility has been reported. A weekend leak-site listing by the Qilin ransomware group has caused some confusion, but it names Cole Manufacturing, an American company, not Coles. Expect scammers to use the news, so treat any unexpected text or email claiming to be from Coles with suspicion; Coles says it will never ask for your password or sensitive account details that way.

Global threats.

A Linux kernel flaw nicknamed NoisyTCP lets an unauthenticated attacker crash internet-facing servers by sending crafted network traffic. This is a different bug from the kernel networking flaw we covered at the end of October. Researcher Hyunwoo Kim found a race in how the kernel looks up established connections when SYN cookies are in use, which leads to a kernel panic. It works against ordinary listeners such as web and SSH servers on machines with more than one network receive queue, or with packet steering spread across processors, which is common on virtual machines and modern servers, and because it is a kernel bug, crashing a web server in a container takes down the whole host. The flaw dates back to 2015 and affects almost all Linux distributions. Red Hat rates it 7.5 out of 10. On virtual machines Kim triggered it in between five seconds and two minutes, while a quiet bare-metal server took about fourteen hours. A second, harder path can corrupt memory, but Kim does not claim general code execution, saying only that he has a very limited lab exploit and is still researching, and there are no reports of attacks so far. Fixed stable kernels are out, and the versions are in the show notes. If you can't patch and reboot yet, the workarounds are to disable SYN cookies in every network namespace, which weakens flood protection, or to serialise traffic to a single receive queue.

Microsoft has published a preliminary post-incident report on last week's worldwide Microsoft 365 outage, according to BleepingComputer, which has seen the report in the admin centre. Microsoft says an update to its networking gateway servers in July carried a latent bug that its load testing missed, because the lab used less powerful hardware with fewer cores. About fifteen hours before the outage, Teams traffic failed over onto the gateways used by Exchange Online, while about fourteen per cent of gateway capacity was already offline for an unrelated reason. That combination triggered the bug, and at its worst more than eighty per cent of the gateway servers stopped responding, with safeguards between regions failing to contain it. Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint could switch to other infrastructure, but Exchange Online could not, which is why mailboxes were out longest. Microsoft has added capacity and safeguards, and a final report is due later this week. The bug sat unnoticed for about four months before it was triggered.

Lithuania's customs agency has partially restored its online services after a cyberattack detected on the 10th of November. The attack forced it to shut down its main user management portal, which people and businesses use to reach customs services and several European Commission systems, including the Import Control System that assesses goods entering the European Union. The agency has confirmed unauthorised access and a possible data breach affecting users who logged in on the 9th and 10th of November, which its chief says could involve several hundred people and businesses, mostly in Lithuania. He told public broadcaster LRT that the attackers appeared to be trying to go deeper into systems holding more sensitive data. No attacker has been named, and the details of how they got in remain unconfirmed.

The European Union has sanctioned 30 individuals and two entities over Russian disinformation and malicious cyber activity. The listings include the Moscow-based Social Design Agency and 24 of its staff over Kremlin-directed influence campaigns, and, under a separate cyber sanctions regime, the pro-Russian hacktivist group NoName057(16), which is blamed for distributed denial-of-service attacks on European government, critical infrastructure and election-related websites. The measures are asset freezes and travel bans.

Ticket seller Fandango is notifying customers that skimming code on its checkout page captured payment card details entered between the 8th and 11th of October. The exposed data includes card numbers, security codes, expiry dates, billing postcodes and email addresses, which is enough for online card fraud. Fandango hasn't said how many customers are affected or how the page was compromised. If you bought tickets in that window, ask your bank for a replacement card rather than waiting for fraud to show up.

A brief update on the Fireworks AI breach we covered yesterday: Uber's breach notifications put the number of affected people at about 150,000, with names, addresses and driver's licence numbers taken through a compromised service provider that TechCrunch reports was Fireworks.

AI security.

Google Threat Intelligence Group's latest AI threat tracker says attackers have moved beyond typing prompts into chatbots to running open-source, multi-agent penetration testing frameworks during live intrusions. It names Artex, Cairn, CyberStrikeAI and PentAGI, and says it has seen Cairn used against targets including the American aerospace sector. Google also describes attackers using large language model pipelines to compare patched and unpatched code and work out what a fix changed, shrinking the gap between disclosure and attack. After two flaws in PaperCut's print management software were first exploited as zero-days, GreyNoise saw an attacker likely use AI to build exploits and launch a mass campaign within four days of disclosure. Other trends include criminals reselling access to stolen AI model credentials, and espionage groups tampering with coding assistant configurations to plant backdoors. The pattern was visible in a disclosure from Barracuda, which says an attacker using Artex entered its cloud environment on the 1st of November with a valid access key issued to a third-party AI vendor that had never been revoked. Over about forty-four hours the attacker touched around ten cloud accounts, harvested secrets from configuration stores and Kubernetes service accounts, searched some servers for cryptocurrency wallet files and left a backdoor in a Kubernetes cluster, which Barracuda has removed. None of this needs a clever model. The agent framework goes after forgotten keys, default passwords and unpatched services, and it works faster than a person. Amazon's threat intelligence team, writing about Artex campaigns it observed, makes much the same point: close off exposed secrets and default credentials, and patch.

Industry and patches.

Google's Project Zero is shortening its disclosure deadlines, citing AI-assisted bug finding and exploitation. Under its new policy, details of a vulnerability are published 90 days after the vendor is told, whether or not a fix has shipped, and the extra thirty days of patch roll-out time is gone. The deadline drops to sixty days on the 2nd of February 2027 and to thirty days on the 3rd of January 2028, with exceptions for bugs already being exploited or already public. Project Zero says the extra month no longer means much, because attackers can now work out a bug's details from the patch almost immediately. Vendors that rely on the grace month will want to start planning now.

Microsoft has also reminded customers that Windows Server 2016 stops receiving security updates when it reaches end of support in about two months. Any server still on it in the new year needs a migration plan, or extended security updates, before then.

Outlook and patch priorities.

First, NoisyTCP: put internet-facing Linux hosts, container hosts in particular, on the fixed kernels and reboot, or apply one of the workarounds until you can. Second, Coles customers and staff should expect Coles-themed phishing in the coming days. Third, anyone who bought Fandango tickets in mid-October should have their card replaced. For cloud teams, revoke access keys issued to third parties that no longer need them and alert on new use of old keys. Four US federal deadlines fall next Tuesday, the 24th of November, for exploited flaws in Microsoft Outlook, Cisco BroadWorks, the Openwave media transcoder and Google Chrome. Watch for Microsoft's final outage report and any further update from Coles.
