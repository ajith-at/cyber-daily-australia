Hello from a cloudy Melbourne Saturday, with showers expected and a high around fifteen. This is U51, your cyber and AI security brief for Saturday the 7th of November, covering the 24 hours to about 7 am Melbourne time.

Here are the headlines. A password bypass dubbed sshole lets attackers walk into passwordless accounts on Alpine and other musl-based Linux systems running OpenSSH without PAM. United States agencies have issued a joint warning on ongoing cyber attacks against industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring. Bitdefender details GhostMap, an Android flaw where installing a crafted app with no permissions can lock a phone in a persistent boot loop. Several Asian airlines are notifying customers after a vulnerability at travel IT provider Amadeus exposed booking-display data. And Zyxel has patched a zero-day used against nearly a thousand GS1900 switches after devices fixed for an older flaw were still exploitable.

We start with sshole. Security researcher Özgün Kültekin of Wakefield found that on Linux distributions built around the musl C library, such as Alpine, an attacker can log into a passwordless account by supplying any password between two hundred and fifty-seven and one thousand and twenty-four characters long, as long as OpenSSH is not using Pluggable Authentication Modules. That is Alpine's default for its standard OpenSSH package. Passwordless accounts store a single asterisk where a password hash would normally go. When someone types a password longer than two hundred and fifty-six characters, musl's hashing function gives up and returns an asterisk as its error. OpenSSH sees an asterisk on both sides and treats it as a match. The attacker needs the name of a passwordless account, and the machine must also have at least one other account with a real password. Alpine's official cloud images are the high-risk case once configured: they are safe on first boot, and images set up through cloud-init are not affected, but the default alpine user is passwordless and can become root without a password, so once any account on the box has a real password, an attacker who can reach SSH can log in as alpine and take over. Alpine containers are only at risk if they run sshd, which many don't. Root has been passwordless on Alpine since about version 3.16, but OpenSSH's defaults refuse password logins for root. OpenSSH developer Damien Miller's advice is blunt: if you run musl without PAM, set PasswordAuthentication to no or disable SSH immediately. The bug is rated eight point one, and the identifier is in the show notes. The fix is OpenSSH 10.6 p2, then restart sshd; Alpine has patched packages for every branch back to 3.16. Buildroot and Yocto systems can be affected, as can OpenWrt devices that replaced Dropbear with OpenSSH. The discovery method matters for how we talk about AI later: Kültekin used AI agent swarms to build sshd across glibc and musl, with and without PAM, then threw about seventeen thousand six hundred unusual logins at them. That is defensive discovery tooling, not a model acting on its own. Damien Miller said OpenSSH rushed the 10.6 p2 release because AI agents have been rediscovering bugs within hours, and the release notes say independently rediscovered AI-found bugs mean they will ship fixes faster rather than batch them.

Australian focus.

Two stories land for Australian operators this morning. First, the cold-chain warning. After the European supermarket refrigeration waves we covered earlier this week, and the French blood-service cold-storage incident, United States agencies released a joint fact sheet on the 5th of November urging operators to harden industrial refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring. iTnews carried it for an Australian audience. The FBI, CISA, and the departments of Health and Human Services, Agriculture, and Transportation say malicious actors are targeting unpatched or internet-accessible electronic temperature and commercial refrigeration controllers in retail grocery and cold storage, with possible impacts that include changing temperature, fire hazards, inventory loss, shortened equipment life, and reduced visibility of temperature. CISA's Chris Butera said every organisation that depends on refrigeration should assume it could be a target. The FBI's Brett Leatherman said the government is seeing these systems targeted across critical infrastructure sectors globally. Mitigations are familiar and urgent: pull controllers off the public internet, change defaults, patch, and retire unsupported legacy gear. Australian grocery, food, pharma and logistics operators with remote cold-chain monitoring should treat that as applying here too. Second, the Alpine and sshole story above is directly relevant for Australian teams that run Alpine cloud images or any musl-based SSH endpoint without PAM. Many Alpine containers don't run sshd; the ones that do, and the cloud images with a passwordless alpine user, need PasswordAuthentication off or the patched OpenSSH build now.

Global threats.

More on that refrigeration campaign. The United States joint product is the significant new development: a multi-agency public warning that the threat is ongoing, not a closed European incident. Prior waves include French supermarket freezers, a second European retail wave, France's national blood service cold storage on the 3rd of November, and mysterious grocery refrigeration malfunctions at United States military bases under investigation. Today's action for defenders is the fact sheet's hardening list, not a re-run of the earlier victim timelines. Details and links are in the show notes.

Bitdefender Labs has published GhostMap, an Android flaw that can leave a phone stuck in a crash loop after a specially crafted app is installed. The app needs no permissions, contains no executable code, and does not need to be launched. It maps a file into unallocated memory; when a core system process reads that region, it gets a bus error and system_server crashes, restarts, and crashes again. Safe Mode also fails. Recovery is a factory reset, which wipes local data, or deleting the file over ADB if USB debugging was already enabled and you win the race before the next crash. Bitdefender reported it to Google in August; Google first closed the report as Won't Fix, Not Reproducible; after a second report with a working proof of concept, it said this was outside the scope of a security update and passed it to a product team for a possible future version. Researchers reproduced it on a Pixel 10 running Android 17 with the 5 August patch, with the crash starting about one second after install, and say it affects Android 10 and above. Bitdefender has not seen the persistent boot-loop technique used in the wild; the original sample that sparked the research was a banking dropper that only crashed the installer. Until Google ships a fix, the practical defence is to refuse sideloaded and untrusted APKs, and to heed mobile-security warnings that flag malformed packages before they are installed.

Several Asian airlines have told customers that booking-display data was exposed through a vulnerability at Spanish travel IT provider Amadeus, not through a breach of the airlines' own systems. Amadeus says certain booking-display data was accessible, that it has found no evidence payment or financial data was accessed, that this was not ransomware, and that it has fixed the vulnerability and enhanced monitoring. Japan Airlines has put the figure at about ninety-two thousand customers; Singapore Airlines about forty-six thousand; Cathay Pacific about fifty-three thousand, with names, booking references and flight details, and in some records passports or other travel documents, frequent-flyer numbers and email addresses. Korean Air, Asiana and Malaysia Airlines have also reported. Expect phishing that quotes real itineraries or booking numbers; Singapore Airlines says it will contact passengers with upcoming flights to change exposed booking references.

Norad, Norway's aid agency, says its Grants Portal was hit in an attack discovered on the 30th of October. Attackers abused twenty-seven user accounts. They may have accessed names and email addresses of all twenty thousand six hundred and fourteen registered users across twelve thousand five hundred and eleven organisations. Information related to grant applications from at least three organisations was compromised, and more may be. The portal has been closed. Norad has notified the Norwegian data protection authority, the National Security Authority, and the police. The security authority says the attack shares characteristics with other recent incidents; perpetrators are unknown. Norad cannot rule out that names of applicants in conflict zones or authoritarian states were accessed, which is the human-risk angle that makes this more than a routine portal breach.

And Zyxel has patched a separate zero-day in GS1900 series switches after attackers had already used it against nearly a thousand devices. GreyNoise tracked a suspected Chinese-speaking actor it calls Kapibala, linked to the Red Heron campaign, compromising about nine hundred and ninety-six devices across forty-eight countries: steal configs, hashed root credentials and network information. The activity was initially blamed on an older pre-auth flaw patched in June. Firmware security firm Binarly found the exploit still worked on firmware that had that June patch, because it was a different out-of-bounds write in the web CGI, reachable with crafted HTTP and no authentication. Zyxel confirmed the new issue in October, shipped firmware on the 22nd of October, and published the advisory around the 3rd of November. Ten GS1900 models are listed; the fixed builds are the 2.90 Patch 3 line, with exact model strings in the show notes. If a GS1900 management interface is reachable on your local network, this is not optional.

AI security.

The sshole find shows how to frame AI in vulnerability research. Kültekin's agent swarms were a harness for combinatorial build-and-test: every mix of C library and PAM setting, then a large set of unusual logins. The agents did not escape anything; a researcher aimed tooling at authentication edge cases and got a reproducible bug. Miller's rush-to-ship comment, and the OpenSSH notes on independent rediscovery, are why long embargoes look riskier now. The same defensive tooling that finds bugs can also mean someone else finds them sooner. That sits beside yesterday's OpenAI defensive pipeline story without repeating it. Discovery capacity is rising, and what counts is the human-reviewed fix path that follows. GhostMap, by contrast, is classic parser-differential research, not an AI story, even if automated analysis pipelines are how malformed APKs often first show up.

Industry developments.

The Amadeus incident is a supply-chain reminder for airlines and anyone else whose customer data lives in a shared booking-display environment. The airlines' own systems were not breached, but customer records still walked out through a vendor vulnerability. Separately, iTnews reports United States regulators are checking bank systems in the wake of the Binance theft we covered earlier this month; treat that as a supervisory follow-on, not a new technical disclosure. And Queens Public Library in New York says ransomware has disrupted its website, PCs, checkout, Wi-Fi, app and digital media, while its sixty-six locations remain open. Public libraries are soft targets with real community impact when they go offline.

Late item. Previdian reports sshole is already being exploited in the wild. Sensors saw the first attempt less than nine hours after the CVE was published, and a Previdian honeypot running real Alpine Linux and OpenSSH confirmed successful root access. One Japan-based source address used oversized passwords, then ran reconnaissance such as id, uname and hostname over non-interactive SSH; eight attempts from that single address on one sensor so far. Previdian has put it on its own known-exploited list; CISA has not added it to KEV. Proof-of-concept code on GitHub is noted as unverified. If you run musl-based OpenSSH without PAM and have not already patched or disabled password authentication, treat this as active scanning, not a theoretical risk.

Outlook and patch priorities.

Critical: musl-based OpenSSH without PAM, especially Alpine cloud images and any container that actually runs sshd, move to OpenSSH 10.6 p2 or Alpine's patched packages and restart sshd, or set PasswordAuthentication to no now, with active exploitation confirmed in honeypots; Zyxel GS1900 switches to the 2.90 Patch 3 builds in the show notes; industrial refrigeration and cold-chain controllers off the public internet with defaults changed and patches applied.

High: WordPress sites should confirm they are on the fixed core release from late October, the version is in the show notes, after CISA flagged active exploitation of the upgrade-window race condition earlier this week; Android devices, refuse untrusted APKs while GhostMap has no vendor security fix; Amadeus-affected airline customers, watch for phishing that quotes real bookings.
