From Melbourne, this is U51 with your cyber and AI threat brief for Thursday the twenty-ninth of October, twenty twenty-six, covering the twenty-four hours to six this morning, Melbourne time.

A suspected China-linked attacker has used PHP to Shell, with help from AI tools, to root firewalls and reach into a US government-affiliated network. Researchers have exposed the covert network Volt Typhoon used for four zero-day campaigns. Tailscale has shipped its Tailworm fixes. And Anthropic says an unreleased model chained browser and operating system flaws into working exploits with little human help.

First, at home. RMS Cloud, the Australian company whose booking software runs more than six thousand hotels, holiday parks and serviced apartments, says it found unauthorised access to a limited part of its environment on Monday. Guest names, contact details and booking details were accessed. RMS says card and payment details were not. BIG4 says scammers are now using the breach. Guests of BIG4, Reflections, G'day Parks and others are getting WhatsApp messages, texts, emails and even automated calls that quote their real booking, then threaten cancellation unless they confirm card details through a link. A Victorian council that runs holiday parks, and operators in Western Australia, have warned their guests too.

The advice is simple. A message that knows your stay dates is not proof it came from the park. Don't click, and call the accommodation on the number from its own website or your original confirmation. If you run a property on RMS, tell guests with upcoming bookings now, and brief your front desk, because they'll get the calls. With summer bookings ramping up, this lure will keep working for a while.

Now to PHP to Shell, and the most serious confirmed use so far. The Israeli security firm Dream says that, in an attack it observed over the weekend, an attacker combined a custom PHP to Shell exploit with a Linux kernel privilege escalation flaw known as Fragnesia, and rooted twenty-five servers in the United States, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea. They weren't ordinary web servers. They were firewalls from a large vendor Dream hasn't named, whose management interface runs an old PHP, version 8.0.30, with a generous memory limit that makes the memory corruption stage workable. Three of those machines sat in a US government-affiliated environment, and from there the attacker went after Active Directory, recovering a service-account password, enumerating users and spraying passwords; Dream can't say whether it got further.

Dream found Anthropic's Claude Code installed in the attack infrastructure, along with an AI-driven offensive platform configured with DeepSeek and Kimi models, and says the style of nearly forty scripts points to AI-assisted development. It links the activity to likely Chinese state-aligned interests because of the four countries involved. All of this comes from one firm's analysis, and the attribution is its own assessment.

Two things stand out. First, this is the answer to the forum chatter that PHP to Shell is too fiddly to use for real, because a determined operator simply built the exploit for one specific product. Second, Dream says twenty-two of the rooted firewalls still have active web shells, and the attacker holds a list of about nineteen thousand fingerprinted targets. Many appliances bundle their own PHP and won't be fixed until the vendor ships firmware. So find out which of your appliances run PHP, keep management interfaces off the internet, and ask vendors for a date.

Staying with China-linked activity, Lumen's Black Lotus Labs has published details of a covert access network it calls Silent Train, which it attributes to Volt Typhoon, the group known for pre-positioning in critical infrastructure. Silent Train was built in late twenty twenty-three, just as authorities were disrupting the earlier KV botnet. Instead of a standing botnet, it uses short-lived chains of hijacked home routers, Internet of Things gear and enterprise appliances, at least twenty-seven device types including TP-Link, Netgear, Zyxel, Fortinet and Cisco, rebuilt for each operation and cleaned up afterwards. The final hop is usually a compromised device inside the United States, to slip past geographic blocking.

Black Lotus Labs ties four zero-day campaigns to it: Versa Director, N-able N-central, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller and Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator. Each is a management or control plane system that, once owned, gives sight of, or control over, a large estate. The network is described as now disrupted, and hundreds of victims are listed across telecommunications, government, defence, water, energy and transport. The lesson for defenders is that IP reputation and geofencing won't stop an operator who arrives through a domestic home router. Give your management platforms the same scrutiny as your edge devices.

Next, a patch we've been waiting on. Tailscale has released client version 1.104.4, fixing the Tailscale SSH forwarding flaws from the Tailworm report. The cause was that forwarding to a Unix socket ran as root after a path check that a timing trick could beat; in the researcher's test, forwarding to the Docker socket produced a root shell. The fixed version forwards as the logged-in user and relies on normal file permissions. Remember the attacker first needs a device your policy already allows to open an SSH session. Update Linux and macOS servers running Tailscale SSH. If you can't yet, set the environment variable that disables SSH forwarding and restart the service, and note that check mode doesn't help here, because the forwarding happens inside an approved session.

A few more for your patch list. Attackers are already probing a critical Oracle E-Business Suite flaw patched in last week's quarterly update, an unauthenticated SQL injection that chains to command execution. Defused Cyber's decoys saw exploratory attempts on Tuesday. E-Business Suite was hammered by the Cl0p extortion crew last year, so if yours faces the internet, it goes to the top of the queue.

CISA has added two more entries to its known exploited list: PolyShell in Adobe Commerce and Magento, which Sansec says has been used to plant a loader on hundreds of stores, and the Brotli decompression flaw, where ELLIO has recorded exploit traffic using public tooling against Node.js. For Node, the fixed releases are 22.23.4, 24.22.1 and 26.12.1, and there's no workaround. US federal agencies have until the tenth of November.

On PeopleSoft, the Zero Day Initiative says the zero-day ShinyHunters has been exploiting since May is really three chained flaws under one identifier, rooted in a server request forgery that reaches an internal admin hub because PeopleSoft trusts its own loopback address by default. Mandiant has seen the group dodge firewall rules by encoding a single letter of the path. The June patch closes the chain; it doesn't evict anyone already inside, so rotate credentials.

And a critical sixteen-year-old flaw in EDK II, the reference code behind most UEFI firmware, lets an attacker on the local network run code before Secure Boot, but only while a machine is booting over the network. There's no fixed stable release yet, so for now, limit who can reach your network boot segments.

Briefly from elsewhere. Moldova's prime minister says the government is examining foreign state involvement in an attack that pulled data linked to about seven hundred and fifty thousand identity numbers from public services. Japan's GMO Internet Group has refused a fifty-million-dollar extortion demand over a breach of its desktop cloud for currency traders, and is offering up to a million dollars for tips leading to arrests. The United States has seized the domain of the bulletproof host Virtualine, with Dutch, German and British police, and has sanctioned Russian and North Korean nationals for laundering the earnings of North Korean IT workers.

On to AI security. Anthropic's Frontier Red Team says an unreleased internal model found flaws in Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux and chained them into five working proof-of-concept exploits after initial prompting. One opened the calculator on a Mac within seconds of loading a page in Chrome. The Linux chain gets root over the network on servers running a multipath TCP listener. Everything was reported and is patched, and Google and Apple credit Anthropic for specific fixes. How much the model did unaided is the company's own claim, and it doesn't say what people did at each step. Put that next to the PHP to Shell story, with Claude Code sitting on an attacker's server, and the gap between finding a bug and weaponising it is narrowing on both sides. Keep browsers and kernels current, and assume patch windows will keep shrinking.

Cequence Security reports a scanner pretending to be thirty-two different AI crawlers and assistants, including ones from OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and Meta, to hunt for exposed password files, API keys and cloud credentials. On one day this month, nearly half of its customers saw the traffic. It apparently found nothing. Still, if you allow traffic because the user agent says it's a friendly AI bot, check the published IP ranges, not the label. Bots, it turns out, can lie about who they are too.

And in Europe, the privacy group noyb has sued Scale AI over what it calls invasive surveillance of the workers who label training data.

In industry and policy, the United Kingdom's budget commits to a consultation next year on where liability for cyber breaches caused by insecure technology should sit. It's one bullet point in a hundred and sixty-six pages, but it's the first formal British commitment to examine making vendors answer for insecure products, with Jaguar Land Rover's estimated one point nine billion pound hack as a backdrop.

Your priorities for the next forty-eight hours. Critical: any appliance or server running PHP, including vendor firmware, and particularly firewall management interfaces, which should never face the internet. Critical too: Oracle E-Business Suite, now being probed. High: Tailscale 1.104.4 on SSH servers; Node.js Brotli fixes where you decompress untrusted input; Adobe Commerce; and credential rotation for PeopleSoft. If you're an RMS customer, warn your guests today.

Looking ahead, expect the firewall vendor in the Dream report to be named, more targeted PHP to Shell exploits for specific appliances, and further phishing waves from the RMS data.
