Good morning from Melbourne. This is U51, your cyber and AI security brief for Saturday the 21st of November, covering the 24 hours to about six this morning, Melbourne time.

Here are the stories leading this Saturday's headlines. Attackers are exploiting a critical Cisco Expressway flaw to get root access and move deeper into victim networks, and the United States cyber agency has added it to its exploited list. Amazon says a suspected China-linked operator's AI agents found and used a previously unknown flaw in a medical imaging tool to break into university servers. The United States Department of Agriculture has taken systems offline after a security incident. Australian financial services firm Bell Financial says some client data was likely accessed. And the White House has a new acting National Cyber Director.

We start with Cisco Expressway, the gateway that lets video calls and collaboration traffic cross the edge of a corporate network. Cisco patched the flaw, CVE-2026-76466, in October and rates it 9.8 out of 10. The database service used for clustering relies on persistent cookies, and when clustering is set to verify certificates, client certificates aren't actually checked, so anyone holding those cookies can connect, call internal commands and run anything they like as root. On Thursday, the Russian security firm F6 reported what it calls the first in-the-wild exploitation, which followed the release of a public proof-of-concept. The attackers compromised two internet-facing Expressway devices, then used one of them to reach two more cluster nodes inside the network. From there they ran custom Python scripts to scan for file shares, talk to SQL Server and Windows remote management, and comb GitLab repositories for credentials. F6 says comments in the scripts suggest they were written with help from AI tools, and it hasn't attributed the activity to any known group. The United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue overnight, with a federal deadline of Monday. There is no workaround. Older releases need to move to a supported branch, and the fixed version and F6's indicators are in the show notes. If your Expressway devices sat unpatched and exposed, hunt for compromise as well as upgrading.

The same catalogue update brings three more additions. One is an older SAP NetWeaver Enterprise Portal flaw, CVE-2025-42884, which lets an unauthenticated attacker read or change server information through injected directory lookup settings. SAP fixed it in 2025, so this is a reminder that old portal bugs still get used. The other two are follow-ups: the VMware vCenter zero-day we led with yesterday and the Apache Roller flaw we covered on Thursday are both now formally listed, also with a Monday deadline. CISA notes that the Roller entry covers a broader range of versions than the original identifier, so check older deployments again.

Australian focus.

Bell Financial, the Australian stockbroking and financial services group, says some client data has likely been accessed in a cyber incident involving unauthorised access to a limited number of its IT systems. The company says the incident has been contained, client assets remain safe and it is operating as usual. It hasn't said when the intrusion happened or what kind of data was involved. Bell has notified the Australian Cyber Security Centre and the Australian Federal Police, has engaged with the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, and is working with specialist advisers. Details are still emerging, and clients should be alert to phishing that uses the incident as a hook.

Global threats.

In the United States, the Department of Agriculture has taken numerous applications offline after its security operations centre uncovered an incident. According to Nextgov and Government Executive, an internal email sent on Monday from the department's farm programs business centre said every database password in every environment there had to be reset, and that was forcing application resets and outages. The disruption has hit financial tools used for payments to farmers, along with loan and aid status systems, and staff were told to expect intermittent outages through the week. It isn't clear who was responsible or whether any data was taken, and this rests on internal documents and people familiar with the matter, not a public statement from the department.

N-able has confirmed that a security incident, not a configuration problem as it first said, caused this week's outage of its Mail Assure and SpamExperts hosted email filtering services across the Americas, Asia-Pacific and Europe. The company says an unauthorised party accessed one server in that hosted cloud, and it switched off the user interface and API while it investigated. It now says the intruder may have been able to reach some credentials stored on the platform, so it has rotated internal credentials, revoked customer API tokens and reset local user passwords. Email flow and spam filtering kept working, and N-able says it has no evidence that customer data or mailboxes were accessed. Managed service providers in this region who resell these services should keep an eye on N-able's updates and review admin access and reissue API integrations once the console is back.

In Ireland, more than 40 credit unions have notified the Data Protection Commission after hackers exploited a vulnerability at their software supplier, Wellington IT, and accessed payee details stored in members' mobile banking apps. The flaw was closed on the 6th of October, but credit unions weren't told until the 16th of November, a six-week gap the Irish minister responsible has called simply not acceptable. Officials say there is no evidence that members' funds were touched, and the United Kingdom's privacy regulator is starting to receive reports too.

Transport for London says around 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had their account details exposed. It found the breach on the 16th of November after customers reported unexpected emails sent from its own mail systems. The photocard website is offline, and TfL says passwords and payment card details weren't affected.

For infrastructure teams, a flaw in KVM, the hypervisor built into Linux, let a researcher at Terra Security read about 127 gigabytes of a host server's memory from inside a DigitalOcean virtual machine. It only affects AMD hosts with nested virtualisation turned on. Fixed kernels are out upstream, DigitalOcean has live-patched its fleet, and anyone running their own AMD hosts should update or turn nested virtualisation off.

A researcher has also published an unpatched Windows lock screen bypass called FastUnlock. It needs physical access and uses the Narrator screen reader to start programs in a limited, non-administrator account. ThreatLocker reproduced it, there is no fix yet, and no attacks have been reported.

AI security.

Amazon's threat intelligence team has published one of the clearest looks yet at an AI agent framework being run by an attacker. In September, it found a public, unauthenticated instance of Cairn, an open-source autonomous penetration testing framework, and could read its whole history: prompts in Simplified Chinese, timestamps set to China Standard Time, and a target list of universities in East Asia outside mainland China. The operator gave the agents a goal of obtaining shell access. Against one open database server, the agents ran 148 actions over about 16 hours and failed. Against a university GPU server, they tried more than 30,000 username and password combinations over more than six hours and failed again. But against MONAI Label, an open-source tool for labelling medical images, they found an unreported path traversal flaw in an upload endpoint and went from mapping its API to writing files on the server in about 11 minutes, then got a root shell. The operator then gave the agents a tunnel into a second university running the same software. Amazon is confident the flaw was a zero-day, and links the operator to China with moderate confidence. The operator set the goal and later handed the agents a tunnel into a second university, but Amazon says the Cairn framework's agents found and exploited the flaw without the operator choosing each step. The maintainers have published an advisory, and the details are in the show notes. Ironically, the operator was caught because they left their own attack console open to the internet. We'll go deeper on agent-driven intrusions in tomorrow's Sunday Special.

Bell Canada has confirmed that what it calls an agentic threat actor breached its systems on Tuesday and took enterprise customer data, including from a pre-production copy of the system it uses to order and provision telecommunications circuits. The exposed data includes business contact details, ticket titles and some IP addresses of network elements, but Bell says no credentials or highly sensitive personal information. Bell hasn't explained what agentic means here or how the attacker got in, so treat the label as the company's description, not a technical finding.

Industry and policy.

National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the White House. Pentagon chief information officer Kirsten Davies has been named acting director. The change comes amid a run of high-profile technology outages in the United States that officials maintain were not caused by a coordinated cyberattack.

SonicWall is telling customers to retire Generation 6 firewalls immediately and take them off the internet edge. The company says these devices won't get security patches, even for critical flaws, and it expects internet-facing units to be actively targeted. Its advice is to migrate to Generation 8 hardware running SonicOS 8.

F5 has released out-of-band hotfixes for five critical BIG-IP flaws in the Access Policy Manager and the iRules scripting feature. In vulnerable configurations, an unauthenticated attacker can crash the traffic engine and possibly run code. There are no mitigations and no reported exploitation. Shadowserver sees more than 14,600 internet-facing addresses showing signs of the access manager. F5's affected and fixed versions are in its advisories, linked in the show notes.

Drupal has fixed a critical flaw in its built-in installer that, in uncommon configurations, could let an anonymous attacker reinstall a site and take it over. A proof-of-concept exists, so update and block the installer page. The fixed versions are in the show notes.

Outlook and patch priorities.

First, Cisco Expressway: upgrade to the fixed release, check for the indicators in the show notes, and rotate any credentials reachable from those devices, including GitLab tokens. Second, the other catalogue additions: SAP NetWeaver Enterprise Portal, vCenter and Apache Roller, all due Monday for United States federal agencies. Third, F5 BIG-IP systems using the access manager or iRules, then Drupal sites and any AMD virtualisation hosts with nested virtualisation turned on. If you run MONAI Label or similar research tools on the internet, take them offline until patched. Over the next two days, watch for more Expressway victims, any detail from Bell Financial on the data involved, and whether the Department of Agriculture explains its incident.
